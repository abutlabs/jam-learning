# What changed: GP 0.7.2 → 0.8.0

<span class="lecture-badge">M1 Understanding · reference</span>

lasair's M1 conformance was proven against Graypaper **0.7.2** (tag `gp-0.7.2-final`).
The current Graypaper is **0.8.0**, and lasair now implements it. This page is the diff
between the two, read chapter by
chapter from `git diff v0.7.2 v0.8.0 -- text/` in the vendored Graypaper (25 commits,
24 files, +1622 −843 lines), and cross-checked against lasair's migration plan in
`docs/GP_0_8_0_PLAN.md`. Everything here is either a substantive protocol change or
explicitly marked cosmetic.

Where a change is cited as `#NNN`, that is the Graypaper pull request number from the
commit log between the two tags.

<div class="callout callout-info">
<div class="callout-title">Where lasair's migration stands (from its migration plan)</div>

As of 2026-09-30, Gates 0–7 are green: pins and red baseline, codec and
types, trie and erasure, the non-PVM STF families, the PVM gas model, traces and the fuzz
protocol, and the live network (lasair follows and co-validates a 0.8.0 PolkaJam
nightly). Phase 8 published `v2.0.0` and `client-v2.0.0` after a pre-release audit that
fixed about 25 GP validity rules no official vector exercises (`docs/TINY_TO_FULL_AUDIT.md`,
fourth sweep). Phase 7 put services and jamswap on 0.8.0. Fuzzing against the public image
(8.1) comes last: the official fuzzer's first 0.8.0 batch against lasair 2.0.0 found four
bugs, fixed on 2026-09-30 (lasair 2.0.1; `regression-reports/README.md`), and the full
lane sweep is still to come. When you describe "how lasair handles it", say which phase it
lands in.

## Where the vectors and the text disagreed

The jam-test-vectors 0.8.0 changelog (PR #112, merged 2026-09-16) has a **Deviations**
section. These are the places where the published vectors intentionally do not follow
the 0.8.0 text as printed, and an implementation must follow the vectors:

- **`grow_heap` out-of-gas drain (GP #533):** on insufficient gas the counter drains to
  zero and the invocation exits OOG.
- **64 KiB stack rounding (GP #538):** the address-space limit rounds the stack
  reservation up to the 64 KiB zone.
- **`bless` from a non-manager (GP #519 / #558):** the 0.8.0 text (#519) answers HUH when
  the caller is not the manager, but the vectors do **not** refuse a non-manager
  (`bless_from_non_manager`: the designator hands its role over). GP #558 will let
  non-manager privilege holders hand over their role, and both changes are to be applied
  together, so lasair keeps the rule behind one predicate,
  `bless_refuses_non_manager = false` in `lib/pvm_host.ml`.

Two more items from the same review are worth knowing because other clients got them
wrong in public: the **`invoke` pre-check** (exit out-of-gas before the inner PVM runs
when the remaining gas is below the host cost plus the inner budget), and the
**GP 14.13 result-code ordering** (check the export count against the declared count
*before* checking whether the result is a blob). Both are encoded as unit tests in the
migration so they are never inherited by accident.

## Question bank

### Q1 ★ Name three substantive 0.8.0 changes without notes, and say which lasair phase each lands in.
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Any three of: per-basic-block gas with a gas-charged flag and the removal of `sbrk`
(Phase 4); the host-call gas table (Phase 4); ρ holding the full guarantee and pending
reports cleared on a set-size change (Phase 1 codec, Phase 3 STF); resizable validator
sets with tickets-per-validator ⌈2E/|set|⌉ (Phase 3); disputes hard limits and no
two-culprit minimum (Phase 3); the per-item preimage leaves in the extrinsic hash
(Phase 1); the slot in recent-history entries (Phase 1 and 3); the vectors' clarification
that the trie discriminator bits are the most significant bits of the head byte (Phase 2,
a vectors-side fix, not a text change).

</details>

### Q2 What did 0.8.0 change about the extrinsic hash, and why does lasair regression-test that function specifically?
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Preimages now contribute as (E₄(service index), Blake2b(data)) pairs so any single
preimage's inclusion can be proven, where 0.7.2 hashed the whole encoded preimage
extrinsic. lasair tests it specifically because that function once hardcoded the
disputes component as three empty sequences, which rejected the first real
disputes-carrying block before ψ was touched; a changed leaf format is exactly the kind
of edit that could reintroduce a silent mismatch.

</details>

### Q3 Why does the 0.8.0 Ψ signature carry a Boolean?
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

It is the gas-charged flag of the new gas model. Gas is charged for a whole basic block
on the first step of execution and whenever execution enters a new basic block or jumps
back to the start of the current one. The flag records whether the current block has
already been paid for, so a host call or a resumed invocation does not pay twice and an
out-of-gas exit happens at the block boundary. The inner-PVM record in Appendix B carries
the same flag for the same reason.

</details>

### Q4 What are the three "Deviations" in the 0.8.0 vectors and what do they tell you about how to treat the text?
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

`grow_heap` drains gas to zero and exits OOG on insufficient gas (#533); the
address-space limit rounds the stack reservation up to the 64 KiB zone (#538); `bless`
does not refuse a non-manager caller, although the 0.8.0 text (#519) answers HUH, because
#558 will relax that rule and both changes are to be applied together. They
show that the text and the published vectors can disagree for a release, that the
vectors are what other clients are tested against, and that an implementation should
isolate such rules behind one named predicate so a later flip is a one-line change.

</details>

### Q5 Which 0.8.0 change most affects the tiny-versus-full audit lasair ran for M1?
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Validator sequences and verdict votes becoming length-prefixed rather than fixed-V. The
M1 audit's L0 lint treated V as a codec width and hunted for constants frozen at the
tiny value (F5, F6 and the F7–F11 class). In 0.8.0 the width is carried on the wire, so
that whole class of bug disappears for those fields, and the lint has to be retired for
them rather than extended.

</details>

### Q6 How does the 0.8.0 accumulation gas limit differ from 0.7.2?
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

In 0.7.2 the prefix of reports chosen for accumulation was the largest whose digests'
gas limits summed to at most the budget, with transfer gas added to the budget
afterwards. In 0.8.0 the check sums three things against the budget together: the
report digests' gas limits, the gas of the deferred transfers being processed, and the
gas reserved for always-accumulate services. The outer function also returns the
processed transfers so per-service statistics can count them. The budget for the next
round is also different: 0.8.0 adds the gas of the transfers this round *created* (they
are processed next round) and subtracts the gas used, g* = g + Σ_{t∈t*} t_g − Σ u
(eq:accseq).

</details>

### Q7 ★ Validator sets can now be smaller than 3C. What does that change when a block imports guarantees and assurances?
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Four things, all from #514. First, only the first |κ'|/3 cores are active. The guarantor
assignment P(v, e, t) shuffles ⌊i/3⌋ over the v validators and R rotates modulo |c|/3,
and a guarantee must name a core below |κ'|/3 and validator indices below |k|, even when
its timeslot is in the previous rotation (eq:guarantorsig). So ρ'[c] is always ∅ for
c ≥ |κ'|/3, which also caps how many reports there are to audit. Second, each incoming
report's availability spec must say it was erasure-coded into |κ'| chunks, one per
assurer (the check just before eq:reportcoresareunused). Third, assurances count against
the prior set: assurer indices are below |κ| (eq:xtassurances), and a core becomes
available with more than ⅔|κ| bits set, that is ⌊2|κ|/3⌋+1, where 0.7.2 used ⅔V
(eq:availableworkreports). Fourth, when |κ| ≠ |κ'| every assignment is cleared, "timed out
early" (eq:availassignmentspostassurancesdef). The GP gives no further reason; the
practical one is that each report's chunks were coded for the set size at the time it was
guaranteed, and ch. 14 notes that serving two set sizes needs two codings and two
different work-reports.

lasair: before the 2026-09-23 audit the availability threshold still used the spec V and
ρ was not cleared on a resize. Both are fixed (`Stf_config.supermajority_of`,
`test/rho_resize_test`; `docs/GP_0_8_0_PLAN.md` ledger loop 6).

</details>

### Q8 What can an importer check about a refinement context in 0.8.0 that it could not in 0.7.2, and how does it check a posterior state root it never stored?
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

The context now carries the anchor's slot and the lookup-anchor's posterior state root
(eq:workcontext, #526). The anchor must match a recent-history entry on header hash,
state root and accumulation-output super-peak, and now also on slot, which β entries
carry since 0.8.0. The lookup-anchor is checked against the ancestor set, the headers
kept for the last L = 14,400 slots: there must be a header h with the context's
lookup-anchor slot and hash, and a header h' whose parent is h and whose prior-state-root
field H_R equals the context's lookup-anchor root (the check after
eq:limitlookupanchorage). That works because every header commits to its parent's
posterior state root, so the child vouches for it. The ancestor set includes the
importing block's own header (eq:ancestors), so the lookup-anchor may be the parent.

lasair: the pre-release audit (`docs/TINY_TO_FULL_AUDIT.md`, fourth sweep) found this
record check incomplete. It now checks the lookup-anchor's slot and its child's prior
state root, and uses the spec's L where it had used 24 even at full.

</details>

### Q9 Beyond the gas model, name three 0.8.0 changes to how a PVM program is decoded or how its control flow is checked.
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

First, validity is checked up front. deblob(p, ı) accepts the blob only if, walking
instruction by instruction from 0, every opcode is valid and set in the bitmask and the
last instruction is a block terminator, and the entry ı is itself a valid instruction.
Otherwise Ψ panics without running anything (the Ψ and deblob definitions at the start of App. A). The 0.7.2 rule that an
invalid opcode behaves as `trap` is gone. Second, static control flow is stricter. An
unconditional static jump (`jump`, `load_imm_jump`, and now `fallthrough`) panics unless
its target is a basic-block start. A conditional branch panics unless both its target and
its fall-through are block starts, whether or not it is taken; 0.7.2 checked only a taken
target. Third, numbering moved. `sbrk` (101) is gone, so `count_set_bits_64` …
`reverse_bytes` move from 102–111 to 101–110, and a new no-argument opcode 2, `unlikely`,
changes no machine state; it only matters to the gas model (it costs 40 cycles, and a
branch whose target or fall-through is `unlikely` or `trap` costs 1 cycle instead of 20). Host-call ids moved too: `gas` stays 0, `grow_heap` is 1, and every other
call is one higher than in 0.7.2 (`fetch` 2 … `provide` 27).

lasair: before the renumbering was applied, its decoder was off by one in the 101–110
group and every 0.8.0 service faulted (`docs/GP_0_8_0_PLAN.md` ledger loop 5).

</details>

### Q10 0.8.0 gives each validator ⌈2E/|γ_P'|⌉ ticket entries so that a small set can fill the accumulator. What stops an author padding the tickets extrinsic once it is full, and what happened when lasair missed it?
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

Every ticket in the extrinsic must survive into the posterior accumulator: n ⊆ γ_A'. The
GP says "it is invalid to include useless tickets" (safrole.tex, right after the γ_A'
merge equation). The accumulator keeps only the E lowest identifiers, so once it is full
a ticket that does not beat the worst one would be dropped, and including it makes the
block invalid. The rule is unchanged from 0.7.2, but the larger allowance (eq:ticketsextrinsic)
exists so that small sets can saturate the accumulator, which is exactly when the rule
bites.

War story (`docs/GP_0_8_0_PLAN.md` ledger loop 7, 2026-09-24): lasair's importer never
enforced n ⊆ γ_A', and its author kept including tickets after γ_A was full. PolkaJam
correctly refused those blocks, and the mixed network split at in-epoch slot 6–7 on every
run. It was isolated by running a PolkaJam follower on an all-lasair chain, which stopped
at the first block after γ_A saturated. The fix is one shared ticket-rules module used by
both importer and author. No official vector covers this rejection, which is why the
fourth audit sweep went looking for other rules like it.

</details>

### Q11 What does the 0.8.0 gas model mean for where a service should verify signatures?
<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

0.8.0 charges each basic block by a simulated CPU pipeline instead of one gas per
instruction, and the crypto code measured on jamswap got roughly four times more
expensive. Measured under GP 0.8.0 (jamswap `docs/THROUGHPUT.md`,
`docs/LASAIR_INTERNALS.md`): one in-PVM ed25519 verification costs about 5.29M gas (about 1.31M under 0.7.2), and one Groth16
verification about 247M (about 56M under 0.7.2). A work-report's accumulate gas for all
its digests together is capped at G_A = 10,000,000 (the check after eq:reportcoresareunused),
so two signature checks already exceed it, while refine gets G_R = 5,000,000,000 per
work-package. Signature and proof checks therefore belong in refine; accumulate should
only fold already-verified results into state.

</details>
